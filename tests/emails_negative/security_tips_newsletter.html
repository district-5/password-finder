Five minutes to a safer account

This month we are looking at passwords: what makes a good one, and why the advice has changed over the years.

1. Length beats complexity

A long passphrase made of ordinary words is easier to remember and harder to crack than a short password full of symbols. Three or four unrelated words is a good starting point.

2. Never reuse a password

If a site you use is breached, any other account sharing that password is immediately at risk. A password manager will generate and remember a different one for every site, so you only need to remember one.

3. Turn on two-step verification

Even if someone learns your password, a second factor stops them getting in. Use an authenticator app rather than text messages where you can.

4. Beware anyone who asks

No legitimate organisation will ever ask you for your password. Not by email, not by telephone, not in person, not ever.

5. Change it when it matters

Routine expiry is no longer recommended. Change a password when you have reason to think it has been exposed, not on a schedule.
